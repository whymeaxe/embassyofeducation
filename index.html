Why diagnosis comes first

A refusal is not solved by changing a few words.

A new application should be based on truthful facts, meaningful developments and a coherent case—not cosmetic edits, generic templates or rehearsed answers.

Reapplying without diagnosis

  • Same unresolved inconsistencies
  • Cosmetic DS-160 changes
  • Generic interview answers
  • No clear material development

After structured review

  • Reconstructed chronology
  • Connected facts and evidence
  • Identified review areas
  • Better-informed timing and preparation
For a 214(b) refusal, the U.S. Department of State explains that there is no appeal for that application. A person may reapply with a new application and fee and should be able to present significant changes in circumstances or additional relevant information.

EOE 6-Lens Decision Readiness Framework™

Every case is reviewed through six connected exhibits.

The purpose is not to manufacture a story. It is to identify where truthful facts connect clearly—and where unanswered questions or inconsistencies may remain.

EXHIBIT 01

Purpose Logic

Does the stated reason for travel, study or business make practical sense for the applicant's profile, timing, duration and circumstances?

EXHIBIT 02

Information Consistency

Do the DS-160, earlier applications, supporting facts, social information and interview recollection align?

EXHIBIT 03

Financial Credibility

Do income, savings, sponsorship, business activity and trip or study costs form a believable financial account?

EXHIBIT 04

Home-Country Context

Do employment, business, family responsibilities and future plans support the stated temporary purpose?

EXHIBIT 05

Travel & Refusal History

What patterns or material developments appear across previous applications, travel history, relatives and refusals?

EXHIBIT 06

Interview Readiness

Can the applicant explain the case truthfully, naturally, briefly and consistently without sounding memorised?
